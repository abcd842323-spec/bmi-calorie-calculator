How it works · Formula

BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height (m)². Example: 70 kg at 170 cm → 70 ÷ 1.70² = 24.2, which is in the normal range.

BMR uses the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, the most widely used estimate of resting calorie burn:

Men: 10 × weight(kg) + 6.25 × height(cm) − 5 × age + 5
Women: 10 × weight(kg) + 6.25 × height(cm) − 5 × age − 161

TDEE = BMR × activity multiplier (1.2 sedentary up to 1.9 athlete). Weight changes by roughly 0.5 kg per week for every 500 kcal/day you eat below or above your TDEE.

WHO vs Asian cut-offs: the WHO standard calls BMI 25+ overweight and 30+ obese. For South and East Asian adults, health risk rises earlier, so the Asian cut-offs used across India and Asia set overweight from 23 and obesity from 27.5. This page shows your WHO category on the gauge and your Asian category in the note below it.

Common questions

Is BMI accurate for athletes and muscular people?

BMI only uses height and weight, so a muscular person can score "overweight" with low body fat, and an older person can score "normal" with high body fat. Treat it as a first screen, not a diagnosis — waist size and body-fat percentage give a fuller picture.

How many calories should I eat to lose weight?

Start 500 kcal below your TDEE (about 0.5 kg/week). Never go below 1,200 kcal/day for women or 1,500 kcal/day for men without medical supervision — this calculator flags targets that fall under those floors.

Why do two calculators give different numbers?

Older calculators use the Harris-Benedict equation; this one uses Mifflin-St Jeor, which studies find closer for most adults. Activity multipliers are estimates too, so expect your real TDEE to sit within roughly ±10% of the figure shown.

Does this work for children?

No. BMI categories and calorie formulas here are for adults (18+). Children and teens need age- and sex-specific BMI percentiles — ask a paediatrician.

Disclaimer: This calculator gives estimates for healthy adults based on the WHO BMI categories, Asian cut-offs and the Mifflin-St Jeor equation. It is not medical advice. If you are pregnant, under 18, have a medical condition, or are planning a large weight change, talk to a doctor or registered dietitian first.
